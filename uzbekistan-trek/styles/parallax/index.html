A childhood dream

As a boy in Uzbekistan, I saw a photo of a student showing his university classmates around his home country. I promised myself I would do the same one day. At Harvard, I did: on the first day we announced the trek, 40 people signed up.

Jakhongir Sadiev

The first Uzbek in my program in 25 years

When I came to Harvard, I was the first Uzbek in my program at the Harvard Kennedy School in 25 years, and I felt I needed to do something with that. At the American universities where I studied, Uzbekistan and Central Asia rarely came up. I wanted to change that, so I decided to bring my Harvard and MIT classmates to Uzbekistan.

Forty sign-ups on the first day

Honestly, I was not sure 20 people would join. On the first day we announced the trek, 40 had already signed up. Eventually, we had around 200 interested participants for the first treks, so we had to make a selection.

March 2025: 45 travelers

The first trek was a huge success. Forty-five graduate students traveled with us. We met ministers, the Governor of Bukhara and the leadership of the Senate, exchanged ideas with students at Nordic University, and celebrated Nowruz together with the President. The feedback from participants, from the university community and from our hosts in Uzbekistan was so strong that we decided to do it again.

Moreover, the trek contributed further: some students did their research and theses on Uzbekistan, some looked for investment opportunities, and some joined efforts with local universities to launch courses.

March 2026: 50 travelers

The second trek was bigger, with 50 travelers, and it covered more policy areas. On the government side, we met the Chairwoman of the Senate, Tanzila Narbaeva; the leadership of the National Agency for Social Protection, Shakhnoza Mirziyoyeva; the Governor of Bukhara, Botir Zaripov; the Minister of Digital Technologies, Sherzod Shermatov; and the Deputy Chairman of the Central Bank, Dr. Nodirbek Achilov.

In the private sector, we discussed renewable energy with the leadership of ACWA Power, Dr. Jon Zaidi; financial markets with Nika Kurdiani of TBC Bank; and technology at IT Park.

What comes next

My goal is to run more treks and open them to graduate students at other universities. We offer a full package for big groups and direct trips for small groups. After two treks, we know what students want from a week in Uzbekistan, and we can help you organize one for your group.

Jakhongir Sadiev

Questions

Common questions

Who can join?

Graduate students, fellows and postdocs. The trek is open to citizens of all countries. Partners can join when space allows. We do not run trips for undergraduates.

Do I need a visa?

The trek is open to citizens of all countries. US citizens can enter Uzbekistan without a visa for up to 30 days (since January 1, 2026).

For other passports, check the official visa page: Visa to the Republic of Uzbekistan.

Is Uzbekistan safe?

The US State Department rates Uzbekistan Level 1, its lowest advisory level, with the advice to exercise normal precautions (advisory issued January 26, 2026). Every group gets a safety briefing before departure.

Are international flights included?

No. Trips are land-only, so you book your own flights.

Uzbekistan Airways flies nonstop between New York (JFK) and Tashkent a few times a week, in about 13 hours. You can also connect through hubs such as Istanbul or Dubai.

What is not included in the cost?

International flights, travel medical and evacuation insurance (required), visa fees if your passport needs a visa, alcohol and personal expenses, and anything not listed as included.

How does payment work?

In three installments: a fixed first deposit, a fixed second deposit, and the remaining balance before departure. Your quote lists the exact amounts and due dates.

Can a trek run outside spring break?

Yes. Most groups travel during a school break, but we can run a trek in any month and for any length.

Will we meet government officials?

The standard program focuses on companies, universities and/or culture (subject to approval). Formal government meetings are not part of it.

Is there a refund if I withdraw?

You can transfer your place to another graduate student with our approval. Refund terms are set out in the booking terms you accept when you pay. We strongly recommend trip cancellation insurance.

Is this run by my university?

No. Groups organize themselves, and Uzbekistan Trek is not affiliated with any university. Please do not present the trip as an official university program.

Big group questions

What if fewer than 10 people sign up?

We can still run your trip as a private small-group trip, or your travelers can join one of our planned departures.

Can we change the route?

Yes. Add days, add Khiva or the Fergana Valley, or spend a night at a desert yurt camp. Your quote changes accordingly.

Who collects the money?

Each participant pays the operator directly through our registration page. Organizers never handle payments.

Small group questions

Can I come alone?

Yes. Many people join a departure on their own. We pair you with another participant for a shared room, or you can book a single.

Who will I travel with?

Graduate students, fellows and postdocs from different universities and countries, with licensed local guides and an Uzbek host (subject to availability).

Can my partner come?

Yes, when space allows.

Why does a private trip cost more per person?

The car, driver and guide are shared by fewer people. The more of you travel, the lower the price per person.
